# Licenses

The audio here is generated by, or published alongside, third-party
text-to-speech models. **Each clip's licence is the one of the model that made
it**, and is recorded per clip in `samples.json`, field `license`.

## Piper

Its voices come from as many separate datasets and are **not under one
licence**. Read from each voice's `MODEL_CARD`, the 172 here are:

- 104 free of conditions (CC0, public domain, MIT, Apache-2.0)
- 32 attribution (CC BY)
- 11 share-alike (CC BY-SA)
- 19 **non-commercial** (CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA)
- 2 **copyleft** (AGPLv3, GPL 2.0)
- 4 with no licence declared by their model card

The exact one for any clip is in `samples.json`. The four with nothing declared
carry no `license` field at all, rather than a guess.

## OpenRAIL-M

The 300 Supertonic clips are under
[OpenRAIL-M](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3/blob/main/LICENSE),
which permits commercial use but attaches **use restrictions that must be
passed on** to anyone who receives the material or its output. Redistributing
these clips means passing those restrictions on with them.

## What was changed

Nothing was re-synthesized from the Piper models: those clips are the samples
rhasspy publishes, and they say that voice's own demo text. Everything else was
generated here, reading article 1 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights
in each language (from [unicode-org/udhr](https://github.com/unicode-org/udhr);
no known copyright restrictions).

Every clip was then processed identically, which counts as a modification under
the attribution licences:

- converted to MP3, mono, 24 kHz, 48 kbps
- loudness-normalized to -16 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP (EBU R128, two-pass)
- 11 clips longer than 25 seconds cut to 25, with a one-second fade
